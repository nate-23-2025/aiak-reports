03

A rebuild with a business purpose

A clearer path from
“I found you” to “let’s talk.”

I would replace the current site with one built around how families choose care and how Dunamis handles the next conversation.

01 / FOUND

Find Dunamis.

Useful local pages that help search tools understand the home and the questions it can answer.

02 / CHOSEN

Feel informed.

Clear care descriptions, real photos and approved team information that support trust.

03 / CONTACTED

Start a conversation.

A simple call or callback request, with a named person responsible for responding.

04 / TOURED

Take the next step.

A clear visit confirmation and a record of what happens next, through the admission decision.

What changes for a family

  • Know which care is offered and how suitability is assessed.
  • Understand the home before picking up the phone.
  • Find the call or visit request quickly on a phone.
  • Know whether a visit is requested or confirmed.

What changes for you

  • Give referrers a clearer resource to send families.
  • Have a tested route for inquiries and a follow-up owner.
  • See which sources lead to conversations and tours.
  • Build on the same domain, with useful pages and contact routes preserved.

My recommendation is a complete rebuild. The care pages, contact journey, search foundation and reporting need one joined plan. I would agree the scope around how Dunamis actually operates.

04

Built for the way families research

The next family may search.
They may also ask AI.

Whether someone types “assisted living in Magnolia” or asks an AI tool how to compare local homes, Dunamis needs clear, accurate information worth finding and reading.

SEO helps people find the right page.

Search is a chance to meet a family while they are already looking for care. A useful local page can bring that family to a relevant answer, then a clear next step.

The current site repeats a broad Texas search description across its pages. I would give each important page a specific purpose and a clear Magnolia connection.

AI needs facts it can understand.

Care types, location, contact details, provider roles and practical answers should agree across the site and public listings. That gives readers and search systems a clearer account of Dunamis.

I would build those facts into readable pages, then maintain them as the home and its services change.

What “ready for AI” means here: clear information, pages that search tools can access, useful answers, and a well-maintained local presence. Hosting supports that foundation. It does not buy a recommendation from ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google.

Google says its AI search features use the same SEO foundations and require no special AI file or special markup. Visibility and recommendations are not guaranteed. Google’s guidance ↗

05

The long-term investment

A growing library of answers.
An asset Dunamis controls.

Think of the website as your digital property. Search brings people to the street. Your care information and reputation help them decide whether to come inside.

The property

One home for your reputation.

Your domain, approved care pages, real photos and family guidance stay together. Future improvements strengthen the same foundation instead of starting from scratch each time.

The foot traffic

More useful doors into Dunamis.

Each well-researched guide can answer a different family question. That creates more relevant ways to discover the home through search, AI tools or a referrer sharing a link.

The reputation on the block

Trust that has something behind it.

Accurate listings, authentic reviews and a clear introduction to the people behind the care support the story your website tells. They need attention alongside the site.

The learning

A clearer view of what brings families in.

Review the searches, inquiries and tours you can actually observe. Use that evidence to improve the next page, guide and follow-up step.

Plant the seed now.
Give it time to take root.

The next couple of months can be used to prepare accurate pages, build useful guides and begin learning from real interest. When Dunamis is ready for its next chapter, that groundwork is already there.

A paid ad is rented attention. This work builds a body of information Dunamis controls and can keep improving. It still needs maintenance, care and time. The value comes from making it useful.

Useful content creates opportunities for discovery. A larger blog alone does not guarantee traffic, an AI citation or admissions. Care guidance needs appropriate review. Google’s helpful-content guidance ↗

06

Start with the foundation

Make the next eight weeks
count for the months after.

With important operating priorities on your plate, a website can feel like one more thing. I see useful preparation that can begin with a manageable first phase.

Start with what can be prepared now. Approve the care facts, organize the content and plan the contact journey. Public wording, availability and launch timing should match what Dunamis is approved and ready to offer.

Weeks 1–2
Get the facts right

Approve the story and the next step.

Confirm services, provider roles, contact numbers, suitability guidance and who responds to inquiries. Review the existing reports and agree what a qualified inquiry and a confirmed tour mean.

What you have: an approved content plan and clear responsibilities.

Weeks 3–4
Build the foundation

Prepare the new site around the care journey.

Build the main care pages, introduction to the home and simple contact route. Check phones, forms, mobile use and existing page addresses. Review everything with you before launch.

What you have: a reviewed site and a tested launch plan.

Weeks 5–6
Answer real questions

Begin a useful family resource library.

Prepare practical guides using questions you and your team actually hear. Add named review, real Dunamis details and a relevant next step. Publish approved material when appropriate.

What you have: useful pages families and referrers can return to and share.

Weeks 7–8
Learn and improve

Review what is being found and what happens next.

Check available search and inquiry records. Improve confusing pages, identify unanswered questions and plan the next guides around evidence.

What you have: a continuing plan grounded in real activity.

See example topics for the first guides
  • Questions to ask when touring assisted living in Magnolia.
  • What families should prepare for a care assessment.
  • Which services Dunamis provides and which it coordinates.
  • What daily life at Dunamis looks like, using approved photos and real details.

Topics are starting points, subject to operator review. Individual care advice and admission decisions stay with qualified people.

This is a proposed work sequence, subject to agreed scope, access and approvals. It is not a promise of launch, rankings, inquiries or admissions within eight weeks. Search discovery and meaningful results can take longer.
