Start here

The current site is not doing the job Dunamis needs.

The eligibility journey ends at a placeholder. Two care pages leave the actual service setting or provider roles unclear. A form warning needs review, and receipt and follow-up need an authorized test. Search directions conflict in ways worth correcting.

I recommend replacing the current site with a complete custom build off WordPress, planned around clear care information, a tested inquiry route and practical outcome records. Correcting individual pages is useful immediate work; the wider recommendation joins the care journey, search foundation and handover in one project.

Useful foundations already exist: the site opens securely, the 23 pages inspected in the sitemap work, contact details are visible, mobile navigation worked at the tested size, and visitor-counting code is present. Preserve useful content, page addresses, contacts and history during the rebuild.

What this audit establishes

  • Observed: directly inspected public pages, source responses or original captures.
  • Likely implication: a supported concern that still needs testing. Business impact is directional, not measured lost revenue.
  • Needs account access: delivery, reports, ownership or outcomes not established publicly.
  • Recommendation: proposed work, not a proven failure.

“High validation priority” means an important unknown needs checking. It does not mean that a failure has been proved.

06 / The proposed work

One rebuild. Clear responsibilities. Checks before launch.

The new site should become a useful business asset: a clearer introduction to the home, a resource for families and referrers, and a maintained foundation for search and AI discovery. The platform change supports the plan; it does not guarantee results.

1. Care content and family guidance

Approve a clear service map before writing pages. Separate direct care from coordinated services. Complete eligibility guidance, assessment steps, care limits and the appropriate contact. Introduce the people behind the care with approved real photos and verified roles. Create a practical family-resource plan from questions staff actually receive.

2. Search and AI discoverability

Give each important page a distinct purpose tied to the real Magnolia location and approved care scope. Correct conflicting page directions and access to public materials. Keep business facts consistent with managed listings. Organize useful guidance so people and tools can locate answers. Add appropriate business information in the page code and ordinary site maps.

Content should earn its place by helping a family. Topic ideas include tour questions, preparing for assessment, approved care-provider roles and a factual look at daily life. Review care guidance with qualified people. Avoid generic mass-produced articles, invented expertise or repeated location pages. No hosting provider or AI file guarantees an AI citation, ranking or admission.

3. Inquiry receipt and human follow-up

Agree minimal first-contact fields, clear request versus confirmation language, assigned recipients and absence coverage. Use authorized nonpatient tests through receipt, acknowledgment and confirmed visit. Keep clinical decisions and individual suitability with qualified staff. A future administrative assistant, if separately scoped, starts with one narrow nonclinical job and appropriate human approval.

4. Measurement that helps the owner decide

Inspect existing reports first. Agree stages: inquiry received, contacted, qualified, tour requested, tour confirmed, tour attended and admission outcome. Record the source where known, keep unknowns visible and reconcile with staff records. Review what brings relevant families, rather than presenting page views as admissions. Keep sensitive details outside marketing reports.

5. Ownership and a careful move

Confirm who controls the domain, hosting, content, photos and current accounts. Keep existing useful addresses or map redirects. Back up the current site, rehearse contact tests, obtain approval and retain a rollback route. Assign a named owner for changes, maintenance, privacy review and the recurring search/content work.

What should be true before launch

  • Care wording is approved. Services, provider roles, suitability guidance and public availability match the operator’s approved facts.
  • Contact works through to a person. Authorized test messages reach the correct recipient, and confirmation and follow-up responsibilities are clear.
  • Key tasks work on phones and with assistive tools. Read, call and request a visit without confirmed obstacles left unresolved.
  • Existing entrances are preserved. Useful old addresses, contact routes and redirects have been checked.
  • Search directions agree. Public pages, preferred addresses, site maps and approved business facts are consistent and inspectable.
  • Loading is measured. Valid comparison tests replace assumptions about platform speed.
  • Reporting has an agreed purpose. The owner can review known sources and inquiry-to-tour movement without exposing sensitive information.
  • The handover is usable. Access, support, updating and recovery responsibilities are documented.

What the first conversation needs to answer

Which services and public claims are approved now? What can be prepared before publication? Who answers a new inquiry? How is a visit confirmed? What reporting already exists? Who controls the current accounts? Who approves care and privacy wording?

07 / Optional technical appendix

Evidence for whoever runs the website.

This part gets technical. You don't need it to act on anything above. It's here if you're curious or want to hand it to whoever runs your site.

Method and limits

Independent public inspection took place October 16, 2026, America/Chicago, with desktop 1280 × 720 and responsive 390 × 844 views. The page sitemap’s 23 HTML pages, public redirects, page source, headers and selected original captures were reviewed. Selected care pages, the homepage, appointment page and robots.txt were fetched again October 19. That recheck confirmed the eligibility placeholder, mixed care wording, sampled HTTP canonicals and broad asset restrictions. It did not repeat every browser test.

No submissions, CAPTCHA solving, calls, private account access or configuration changes occurred. The PageSpeed request returned a shared-quota error. No valid Lighthouse, field-vitals, search-ranking, traffic, admissions or revenue baseline was established. Public code fingerprints identify implementation clues, not a reliable installed-component inventory or working backend. The public state-directory snapshot corroborated identity at the matched Magnolia address; this audit does not determine current licensing or operating permissions.

Contact and form evidence

The forms are same-origin custom-theme iframes. The appointment form requires name, address, city, ZIP, email, phone, contact preference and best callback time; preferred date/time and comments are optional. The referral form collects referrer and referred-person contact details. No diagnosis, DOB, medical-history or attachment input was observed. Consent and privacy links already exist. The October 16 rendered capture shows the reCAPTCHA Enterprise quota warning. Server-side validation, destinations, storage, access, acknowledgment and receipt remain untested.
